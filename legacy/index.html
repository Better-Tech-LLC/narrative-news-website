Big Tech's Immunity Ends: The Verdict, the Layoffs, and the AI Arms Race

Meta and YouTube are found liable for addiction. Meta cuts 700 jobs and bets $135B on AI. And the model releases keep accelerating.

This may be the week that the social media era's legal reckoning officially began. Juries in two separate courts — New Mexico and Los Angeles — found Meta and YouTube legally responsible for social media addiction and mental health harm to minors. A California jury determined that both companies were negligent in a case brought by a young woman who alleged the platforms served her harmful content from a young age, contributing to anxiety and body image disorders.

The combined damages of $381 million barely register on Meta's balance sheet. What registers is the precedent. This is the first time major platforms have been found liable — not just accused, but found liable by juries — for the addictive design of their products. Legal analysts are calling it a watershed. Thousands of similar cases are waiting in courts across the country. If this pattern holds, the financial exposure is measured in the tens of billions.

Meta's response has been telling. In the same week as the verdict, the company laid off approximately 700 employees across Reality Labs, recruiting, sales, and Facebook teams. But this isn't a company in retreat. Meta guided to up to $135 billion in AI-related capital expenditure for 2026 and revealed four new generations of custom AI chips — the MTIA 300, 400, 450, and 500 — to be deployed across its data centers by the end of 2027. Zuckerberg is making a calculated bet: the future isn't social media, it's AI infrastructure.

He's not alone in that bet. Atlassian laid off 1,600 employees this week — 10% of its global workforce — and replaced its CTO with two AI-focused CTOs. Apple is rolling out a completely reimagined, AI-powered version of Siri with iOS 26.4. The AI Accountability Act passed in the US, requiring bias audits for AI used in hiring, lending, healthcare, and criminal justice.

And the models keep coming. March 2026 has already produced more major AI releases than most entire quarters in 2024. OpenAI shipped GPT-5.4 with a 1.05 million token context window — the largest they've ever offered commercially. Alibaba released Qwen 3.5 with native multimodal support. At least 12 major models and tools shipped in the first week of March alone. The pace is accelerating, and the regulatory infrastructure is struggling to keep up.
